# Scope decisions

Status: **proposed by developer, not yet acknowledged by client.**
These must be confirmed in writing (scope letter) before code starts.

## In scope — 8 weeks

- **Admin + onboarding** — CRUD for all 4 profile types, invite links by SMS, phone-OTP login, active/inactive, NEXT3 ID mapping
- **Expert module (full)** — claim notification, claim detail view (visa, policy, plate, insured, phone, car make/model, city), "Arrived" button writing date/time/location back to NEXT3, photo capture, voice note, car body damage diagram, the 4 document buckets, quality check with retry, search past claims by visa or plate, expert report upload
- **Garage + Claim Officer survey flow (full)** — new claim declaration, upload, officer notification, visa search in NEXT3, approve/reject with comments, approval rendered as an image into the NEXT3 Survey folder, garage notification, post-repair uploads (discharge, invoice, repair photos)
- **Broker Option 1** — form (insured name, insurance type, address, car value, estimated premium, effective date) + document upload/capture, submit, email routed to the AXA recipient for that insurance type
- **Broker Option 2** — **added 2026-08-19 at the developer's decision.** The broker sends a link to the customer's mobile number; the customer completes the same fields, uploads supporting documents, and captures the four car sides plus roof with side selection and a clarity check; the completed file returns to the broker's profile as *ready to send*; the broker reviews and triggers the email. Builds on Option 1 — same fields, same insurance-type list, same email routing.
- **NEXT3 integration** against client-provided API endpoints

> **Consequence of taking Option 2 in scope, recorded honestly:** it adds an estimated **1.5–2.5 weeks** to an 8-week plan that already covered only ~60–70% of the BRD, and it was the primary negotiating chip — that chip is now spent. It is also the project's largest security surface: a public, unauthenticated page collecting identity documents from members of the public.
>
> **How it was paid for (decided 2026-08-19, see `design.md` §11):** the calendar holds at 8 weeks. **Damage-diagram polish and the second UAT round are dropped**; the rest is absorbed by reusing the expert capture/clarity/upload pipeline for the public flow. **There is no remaining descope lever** — any further slip (late NEXT3 sandbox, InfoSec review on our clock, NEXT3 vendor delay) moves the date day-for-day, and that is said to the client now.

| Arbitrating a reclaimed outbox row (the lease creates a two-owner window §6.3 never had) | **`attempts` is the concurrency token.** A reclaim increments it, so a stale worker's outcome write carries `AND attempts = <what it claimed>` and matches nothing. | A row that repeatedly outlives its worker | **Retired to `failed` by the dequeue itself**, via `@max_attempts`. | FIFO draining of the outbox (§6.3's SQL has no ordering either) | **Not implemented, and not a simple oversight.** A CTE with `ORDER BY created_at` makes the sort materialise the candidate set, which locks the rows `READPAST` exists to let a second worker skip; the non-blocking test goes from passing to timing out. Ordering among rows that are all already due matters less than concurrent drain, so it loses. Revisit only with an execution plan in hand — an index supporting the order may allow both. **Consequence to know:** under a backlog an approval PNG can reach NEXT3 after repair photos queued later. *Added 2026-08-20, slice 2.2.* | — |
| Lease expiry depends on the worker's clock, not the database's | **Accepted.** `@now` drives both eligibility and the lease deadline, so a replica whose clock runs more than `LeaseSeconds` fast would reclaim rows nobody abandoned. Single tenant, `minReplicas: 1`, NTP-synced hosts, and — since the concurrency token landed — the worst case is a duplicate push that `clientRef` already dedupes, not a corrupted status. A rejection keeps "Working…" — it is one call with nothing to narrate. *Added 2026-08-22, slice 4.4 (P2).* | — |

| What a garage may attach, and when | **The bucket set is a function of the state.** The two pre-decision buckets are accepted while `decided_at` is null — **draft *and* submitted** — and refused `409 declaration_already_decided` after; the three G4 repair buckets are accepted at `repairs_in_progress` only and refused `409 repairs_not_in_progress` everywhere else; `approval_image` stays refused to a garage outright (4.1's rule, `400 bucket_not_allowed_for_caller`). Slice 5.1's card said "draft →", which would have narrowed today's behaviour and answered a *submitted* upload with a code that is untrue of a declaration nobody has decided — and would have stopped a garage adding the photograph it forgot while an officer reviews. Because the refusal now depends on the bucket, and the bucket is not known until the multipart metadata has been read, the pipeline takes a **`BucketGate` callback** in place of the flat allow-list; the officer's single-bucket case is the same callback built by a helper. *Added 2026-08-24, slice 5.1.* | — |
| What "submit repair documents" requires | **At least one document from any of the three repair buckets** — not specifically an invoice. The `G3Repairs` artboard proposed invoice-only; the BRD says "documents such like discharge, invoice", so singling one out is an invented rule (pass-2 review decision 5, decided here as that review asked). The screen disables the button on the same set, and the server refuses `409 repair_documents_required`. Checked over the *repair* buckets rather than all documents, because every declaration in this state already carries its survey paperwork and the approval image — a check over all of them would pass with nothing from the repair at all. *Added 2026-08-24, slice 5.1.* | — |
| When repair paperwork reaches NEXT3 | **At upload, not at the submit.** The three G4 buckets carry `PushTiming.Immediate` because `repairs_in_progress` guarantees a visa (`CK_declaration_decision`), so each file queues its own push as it lands and the terminal transition sends nothing — it records that the garage considers the job finished. This is also the first garage screen where `PushIndicator` says anything: everything before approval is `deferred`, and 4.4 (P2) deliberately renders nothing for that. *Added 2026-08-24, slice 5.1.* | — |
| An upload that is still streaming when the declaration moves | **Refused with `409 declaration_changed`, not stored.** The gate reads the declaration and the document row commits when the file has finished uploading — seconds later on a phone — so an upload admitted before a decision could land after it as a `deferred` row nothing would ever enqueue: never pushed, absent from A2, blob retained for ever, nothing thrown. The upload's own transaction now carries `state`, the token every transition already uses. **Known residual gap:** the microseconds between approve reading the deferred set and committing it are still open (5.1 moved that read to *after* the NEXT3 verification, which removed the wide part — a call with a 30-second timeout). Neither window is reachable through `WebApplicationFactory`, which buffers the whole request body before the endpoint runs, so both are argued rather than red/green tested. The permanent fix is a **re-queue sweep** for any `deferred` document on a declaration that already has a visa — carried as a ticket. *Added 2026-08-24, slice 5.1.* | — |
| What "Sent to AXA" beside a document means | **A read-time join against the outbox, not a column.** `PushIndicator` has tested `pushStatus === 'sent'` since pass 1, and the API can never produce it: §4 keeps live push state on the outbox row alone, so `document.push_status` reads `queued` from upload until §7.3 deletes the bytes a week later. The string was unreachable on every screen. `DocumentDto` now carries `pushConfirmed`, computed by composing `OutboxSentQuery` into the document lists — the same structural join §7.3's cleanup uses, so nothing outside `Api.Outbox` touches an outbox row and there is still exactly one copy of the fact. Found by the manual pass, which is where it could only have been found. *Added 2026-08-24, slice 5.1.* | — |

| Whether a broker's Option 1 request is emailed inside its own transaction | **No — the state commits first, then the email is sent.** A send inside the transaction rolls a filed request back because a mail server was unreachable, which is work the broker has to do again with the customer still in front of them. Committing first means the worst case is `submitted` with `emailed_at` null, which B1 shows as "email not yet sent" and offers **Resend** for — visible rather than silent, and rather than a lie in the other direction. Same argument §5.2 makes for the officer fan-out. *Added 2026-08-24, slice 5.2.* | — |
| Whether an Option 1 draft's six details can be edited | **No. A mistake means a new request.** The card's endpoint set has no `PATCH`, and a document has to attach to a request that already exists — so B2 is two screens (details, then documents + Submit) mirroring G2 → G3. It is the rule pass 3 already applies to B4 ("a wrong submission means a new link"), applied to the broker's own form. *Added 2026-08-24, slice 5.2.* | — |
| When a broker's documents may be attached | **While the request is a `draft`, and not afterwards** (`409 request_already_submitted`). The submit builds the email from whatever is attached at that moment, so a later file is one nobody receives and nothing ever will — and §7.3 would then delete its bytes on the send's retention clock. The broker equivalent of 4.1's stranded `deferred` row. *Added 2026-08-24, slice 5.2.* | — |
| When a broker document's bytes are deleted | **`Retention:BrokerBlobDays` after `emailed_at`, not after a state.** Option 1's terminal state is `submitted` rather than `sent`, and `expired` is computed in B1's projection and never written, so neither is a column a sweep can test; `emailed_at` says the thing that licenses the delete — the documents have left as attachments — and keeps a failed send's blobs so Resend still has them. Retention for an **abandoned or expired Option 2 request is deliberately not built**: it carries no documents today, and how long a member of the public's identity documents are kept is a client answer (#4/#22). *Added 2026-08-24, slice 5.2.* | #4 / #22 |
| Where the Option 1 upload kill-switch is applied | **Once, over the §7.1 table** (`IBucketRuleOverride`), so the upload's refusal and `GET /api/config/media`'s projection cannot disagree. The bucket rule stays static — §7.1 records what the BRD permits — and `Broker:AllowUpload` is dynamic configuration laid over it, which is what lets B2's file control **disappear rather than grey out** without a release. *Added 2026-08-24, slice 5.2.* | — |
| What currency a car value and an estimated premium are in | **None is shown.** The BRD names both fields and no currency, so `MoneyField` renders the amount alone; drawing a symbol would be an invented client literal, and the wrong one on a policy amount is worse than none. Raised as #47. *Added 2026-08-24, slice 5.2.* | #47 |
